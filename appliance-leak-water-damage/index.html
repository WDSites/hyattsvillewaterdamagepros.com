How a Small Appliance Leak Becomes Water Damage

Appliance leak water damage is moisture left in floors, walls and cabinets after a washing machine, dishwasher or refrigerator releases water. The leak itself may be a cup a day or a hose that lets go all at once. Either way, the water rarely stays where you can see it.

Appliances sit against walls and on top of subfloor, so water slides under the unit, along the baseboard and into the cavity behind it. A washer hose that fails on a Friday can run all weekend. By Monday the laundry room floor is warped and the ceiling below is stained.

This page covers water that comes from the machine. If the damage is mostly in cabinets and counters, the kitchen as a room has its own page on this site.

First-Hour Safety Steps

Water, outlets and heavy machines

Appliances run on electricity, and the outlets are usually low on the wall. Treat any wet outlet as live.

  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet is dry and you are not standing in water. If the outlet is wet, stay out and call 911 and your electric utility.
  • Close the supply valves behind the washer or under the sink. If a valve is stuck, shut the main water valve for the house.
  • Do not tilt or drag a full washer or refrigerator. They are heavy and can pin a hand or crack a floor.
  • If you smell gas near a gas dryer or range, leave and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside. The Washington Gas leak line is 844-WASHGAS, option 1.

Where the Water Goes Under and Behind Machines

Each appliance leaks differently, and the hiding spots differ with it.

Washing machine

Rubber supply hoses and the drain standpipe are common weak points. Water runs under the machine, through the subfloor seam and into the ceiling or room below.

Dishwasher

A slow door seal leak soaks the cabinet base next to it and the subfloor beneath. Toe kicks and adjacent cabinet bottoms often swell before the floor shows damage.

Refrigerator ice maker line

The thin supply line behind the unit can drip for weeks. Water collects under the fridge, where no one looks, and can reach hardwood or vinyl planks.

Upstairs units

In a multi-story building, a leaking laundry closet can wet the ceiling, wall cavities and light fixtures in the unit below.

Upstairs Neighbors, Landlords and Renters

The Census Bureau's QuickFacts lists the Hyattsville owner-occupied rate at 48.4 percent for 2020 to 2024, which means roughly half of households rent. That makes an upstairs appliance leak a real local case. The source of the water may be in a neighbor's unit while you are the one with the stained ceiling.

Document the damage with photos and dates, and tell your landlord or property manager in writing. The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that renters policies cover your belongings, not the building, so your own policy usually handles your furniture and electronics while the structure is the landlord's matter. Call the line for drying in the wet areas you can access.

How Hidden Water Gets Dried

  1. 1

    Stop and extract

    After the source is closed, wet vacuums and extraction units pull standing water from the floor.

  2. 2

    Map the moisture

    A moisture meter checks the subfloor, baseboards and drywall. A thermal imaging camera shows cooler wet zones behind walls and under the machine's footprint.

  3. 3

    Open what holds water

    Toe kick strips, a baseboard or a small drywall section may come off so air can reach the cavity.

  4. 4

    Dry with air movers and dehumidifiers

    Air movers push air across surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room. Readings are checked daily until the materials return to dry reference points under the S500 standard.

Does Insurance Cover an Appliance Leak?

The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies do not cover damage from a slow leak. A hose that bursts and floods a laundry room is sudden. A seal that dripped for months is gradual, and the two can be treated very differently. The MIA also says many policies will not pay for repair of the failed part itself, so the damage is what a claim may address, not the new hose.

Maryland's COMAR 31.15.07.03 lists failing to acknowledge a claim notification within 15 working days as an unfair practice, with exceptions. Report promptly, keep the failed hose or part, and take photos before the cleanup starts. Your policy decides what is covered, so read it or ask your insurer.

Mistakes to Avoid and Easy Prevention

  • ✓Do not push the washer back against the wall until the area behind it is checked and dry.
  • ✓Do not ignore a musty smell near a laundry closet. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and that mold will not grow without moisture.
  • ✓Replace old rubber washer hoses before they fail, and close the supply valves when you go away for several days.
  • ✓Pull the refrigerator out once a year and look at the ice maker line and the floor under the unit.
  • ✓Do not run a dishwasher that is visibly leaking, even for a short cycle.
