Water damage restoration in Hyattsville starts with one call

Pipes burst, roofs leak and basements flood at all hours. If you need water damage repair in Hyattsville, MD, the first job is to stop the damage from spreading. The 24/7 emergency line, (301) 936-0619, is open day and night.

A dispatcher asks what happened, where the water came from and how far it has spread. Then we explain the scope and the price before work starts. Licensed and insured restoration professionals are sent to your door, with same-day response when available.

Time matters because wet drywall, pad and framing break down fast. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. That is why water mitigation comes first: air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and moisture meters go in before any rebuilding.

Hyattsville homes

Older buildings, older pipes

The Hyattsville Historic District was listed on the National Register in 1982. The register entry says the town grew mostly after the 1870s because of rail and communication links between Washington and Baltimore. The city says the district holds nearly 1,000 buildings.

Buildings from that era can have older supply lines, older drains and wall cavities that are hard to inspect. A slow leak can hide inside those wall cavities for months. Thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters help crews find wet spots without tearing open every wall, which helps keep the scope of water damage repair tight.

Water categories

Three kinds of water and what each one changes

The S500 standard sorts water into three categories. The category decides what can be dried in place and what must be thrown out.

Water categories in plain words
CategoryIn plain wordsCommon sourcesWhat it means for the job
Category 1Clean source waterSupply line, sink overflow, rainwater from a clean sourceMost materials can be dried in place if you act fast
Category 2Significantly contaminatedWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, some toilet overflowsCarpet pad is usually removed, hard surfaces are cleaned and treated
Category 3Grossly contaminatedSewage, floodwater, river or creek overflowPorous materials come out, and trained crews do the work

Clean water left alone can slide into Category 2 and then Category 3 as it sits. That is another reason to call early.

Storm drains and sewer lines

Hyattsville downpours and basement water

The city's own Lower Ward 1 Resilient Stormwater Systems Study looked at flood risk. It says 41st Street has standing water during and after intense rain. It also says much of the storm drain system dates from 1960 to 1990.

The study used two storms to build its model: August 2014 with 2.96 inches over about 5.5 hours, and July 2017 with 4.21 inches over about 15 hours. The lesson for a homeowner is simple. A basement near a low street or a clogged inlet can flood in a hard rain even when no river overflows. Flood damage restoration crews handle that kind of loss.

Water and sewer service comes from WSSC Water. Its website lists a 24-hour emergency line, 301-206-4002, for a water main break or a sewer backup into your home. For a backup, WSSC says the homeowner maintains the pipe from the property line to the building. Call WSSC about the main, then call us about the damage inside.

Job size

How the size of the loss shapes the plan

Every job is different, and we do not guess at prices over the phone. These four levels show what drives the scope and the cost.

Small, clean-water loss

Scope: one area

A supply line drip or an overflow that was caught early. Drying often takes a few days with air movers and a dehumidifier.

One-room Category 2

Scope: one room

An appliance discharge soaks one room. Pad and low drywall may come out, and the surfaces are cleaned and treated.

Multi-room or basement

Scope: several rooms

Water travels under walls and into flooring. Extraction units, many air movers and daily moisture readings are needed.

Whole level or sewage

Scope: whole level

Category 3 water or water across a full floor. Containment, heavy removal and a longer rebuild are likely.

Water category, affected area, materials, drying days and rebuild scope set the final price. You hear it before work starts.

Route 1 and renters

Route 1 buildings, shared walls and rentals

Hyattsville runs along the US 1 corridor. The Hyattsville Armory at 5123 Baltimore Avenue sits on it, and the state-designated Gateway Arts and Entertainment District follows it from the Riverdale Park border south. In any building with shared walls and ceilings, water from one unit can travel into the next.

The Census says 48.4 percent of Hyattsville homes are owner-occupied, so about half of households rent. If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager right away and keep your own photos. Renters policies cover your belongings, not the building, according to the Maryland Insurance Administration. Apartment water damage cleanup often involves the building owner, so the order of calls matters.

Insurance and your water damage claim

Check your policy and ask your insurer. The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies do not cover flooding. Sewer or water backup is an optional endorsement that insurers must offer.

Sudden and accidental damage, like a burst pipe, is often covered. Slow leaks are a different story, and most homeowners policies will not cover damage from one. Take photos and video before you move anything, and keep receipts. Ask for a written scope of work, because your insurer will want one.
