When stormwater and sewage come through the floor in College Park

College Park's best-documented water problem is a basement problem. City council minutes record that during the 10 September 2020 rain, stormwater flowed into Calvert Hills houses and sometimes sewage backed up through toilets, drains and sinks. That mix is why a wet basement here is treated as possibly contaminated until a technician has checked it.

Your first moves are the same wherever the water comes from. Stop the source if it is inside the house, stay out of standing water, and call (301) 936-0619 for dispatch at any hour. If the water is rising from a drain, WSSC Water keeps a 24-hour line for sewer backups at 301-206-4002, and it advises starting with a WSSC-registered plumber.

Do these four things while the water is still spreading

Find the shutoff

Supply lines and water heaters stop at the main valve, usually where the pipe enters the house near the meter. Close it before you start carrying anything.

Power only if the panel is dry

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only when the panel is dry and reachable without stepping in water. If the panel is wet, stay back and call your electric utility or 911.

Get belongings off the floor

Lift boxes, electronics and upholstered pieces onto tables or stairs. Soaked carpet and drywall can wait for the crew, but photographs and paperwork cannot.

Treat drain water as sewage

Water that came up through a toilet or floor drain may carry bacteria. Keep children and pets out, then call (301) 936-0619.

What Calvert Hills, Lakeland and Old Town show about risk

Calvert Hills: storm drains still being rebuilt

The city is building a Calvert Hills storm drain project to carry more surface runoff and cut the frequency of significant flooding. Construction mobilization was underway in September 2025, with parts C and D expected in the first quarter of 2027 and parts A and B in the first quarter of 2028.

A county fact sheet on the project says it is not intended as a complete flood solution. Until the work is finished, keep gutters, window wells and floor drains clear before heavy rain.

Lakeland: between two creeks

The county's flood history page lists the Lakeland section of College Park among the places flooded by Tropical Storm Agnes in June 1972. A 1972 newspaper account kept by a Lakeland heritage archive puts the neighborhood between Paint Branch and Indian Creek.

The county's flood-damage study also names Indian Creek at U.S. Route 1 and Paint Branch at Cherry Hill Road as priority areas, though it does not show them inside city limits. Check the FEMA flood map for your address at msc.fema.gov before you decide what coverage to carry.

Old Town: older houses, mostly rented

Old Town's National Register district covers homes built between 1889 and 1965, and the university's off-campus housing office says roughly four in five are rented. A slow leak in an older house can run for weeks before a tenant reports it, and Census data show only 34.0 percent of College Park homes are owner-occupied. Renters should report a leak to the landlord in writing the day they find it.

Matching the service to the College Park loss

The likeliest problems here start with water entering from below, so the list runs from sewage and floodwater to repairs.

Backup or flooded basement

Standing water comes out first with emergency water removal, using extraction units, then drying begins. Porous materials that touched sewage usually cannot be saved.

Creek-side or storm flooding

Rising water from rain events falls under flood damage restoration, which adds contamination handling and a longer drying plan.

Wet walls after the water stops

Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras find water inside walls, and water mitigation uses air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry it to the standard, ANSI/IICRC S500.

Rebuilding afterward

Once structure and contents are dry, water damage repair replaces drywall, flooring and trim. EPA advice is to dry wet areas within 24 to 48 hours to head off mold.

Apartments along Route 1 and near campus

The University of Maryland, founded here in 1856, has more than 41,000 students, and the university's own writers note that the apartment buildings along Route 1 have grown taller. In a stacked building, one failed supply line can wet several units below it.

If water is coming through a ceiling, tell the property manager and call the line at the same time. Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, and have the scope and price explained before work starts.

Who owns which pipe, and who permits the repair

Know the lines before you call

WSSC Water is the water and sewer utility for College Park, and its backups page says the owner maintains the sewer line from the property line to the building. City minutes from a meeting with WSSC show the utility saying stormwater flooding is outside its responsibility.

Insurance treats these differently. Sewer backup often needs an endorsement, flood is usually excluded, and a slow leak is often not covered, so ask your insurer before you assume anything.

For rebuilding, the county's inspection table lists Prince George's DPIE for building and electrical permits and WSSC for plumbing. The city requires its own permit for fences. Ask DPIE before you rebuild walls, wiring or pipes.
