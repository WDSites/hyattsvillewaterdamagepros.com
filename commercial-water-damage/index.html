What commercial water damage covers

Commercial water damage restoration in Hyattsville, MD is the removal of water and the drying of a business property after a leak, a burst pipe, a sprinkler discharge or a storm. The steps are the same as in a house: stop the water, extract it, dry the structure and record what happened. The difference is scale, shared systems and the pressure to reopen.

The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Same-day response is offered when available, and no arrival time is promised. Scope and price are explained before work starts, in writing, so an owner or property manager can weigh the plan before approving it.

Extraction is covered in detail under emergency water removal, and the drying phase under structural drying.

Safety steps in the first hour at a business

Business safety checklist

Keep staff and customers out of wet areas until the water source and the power are dealt with. Slips and shocks cause more injuries than the water itself.

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main valve. If a fire sprinkler line is the source, call the fire protection company, since that system may need to stay charged.
  • Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Call your electric utility or 911 for any sparking or buzzing.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside.
  • For a water main break or sewer backup into the building, WSSC Water lists a 24-hour emergency line at 301-206-4002.
  • Move inventory, paper records and electronics off the floor and into a dry space.
  • Take photos and video before anything is moved, and note the time you discovered the loss.

How a commercial drying job is handled

  1. 1

    Call and triage

    The dispatcher asks what the source is, whether it is stopped, how large the area is and whether the space can be shut for a day or more.

  2. 2

    Survey and moisture map

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show where water has spread under flooring, into walls and across ceiling tiles.

  3. 3

    Extract standing water

    Extraction units pull water from carpet, tile and concrete. Contaminated water is handled differently from clean water.

  4. 4

    Dry the structure

    Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and, in cold spaces, desiccant units run on a schedule. Daily readings are logged. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for this work.

  5. 5

    Document the loss

    Photos, moisture logs and equipment records go to the owner. They help when an insurer asks what happened and when.

Business insurance and records

General guidance only

Commercial property policies differ a great deal, so read yours or call your agent. Sudden and accidental water damage is often treated differently from a slow leak or flood, and business interruption or tenant improvements may be separate items. Some policies set a notice deadline.

The company does not bill your insurer or promise a claim will be approved. It can provide photos, moisture logs and a written scope that you can send to your own insurer. Keep receipts for emergency steps and note any time the business was closed.

Mistakes that prolong a business closure

The usual delays are about communication, not equipment.

  • ✓Waiting for the landlord to respond before calling for help. Mold needs only moisture, and the EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours.
  • ✓Moving inventory around without photos. Later, the insurer will ask what was damaged and where.
  • ✓Opening for business over wet carpet or tile. Wet floors are a safety risk and hide water spread.
  • ✓Treating a sewage loss as ordinary water. That belongs under water damage cleanup.
  • ✓Ignoring the neighbors. A leak that crosses a shared wall may need a joint plan with the unit next door. The same approach applies in College Park and Bladensburg.
