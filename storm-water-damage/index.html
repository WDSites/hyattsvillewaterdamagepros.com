What Storm Water Damage Means in Hyattsville

Storm water damage is water that enters a home during a storm through windows, doors, walls, gutters or yard drainage, rather than rising from a river or through the roof. It covers wind-driven rain pushed into small gaps and downspouts that dump water against a foundation.

If water is inside now, call (301) 936-0619. The line runs 24/7, and crews respond the same day when available. Stay clear of wet electrical outlets, and move anything valuable up off the floor.

Two other pages handle neighboring problems. Water coming through the shingles belongs to roof leak water damage, and rising water from a creek or sewer belongs to flood damage restoration.

Signs Water Came In Sideways

Wind pushes rain horizontally, so water finds places a gentle shower never reaches. Look for wet trim under windows, a damp patch low on one wall facing the weather, bubbled paint around a door and a musty smell in a room that was dry yesterday.

A tree limb that hit a window or siding can leave openings you cannot see from inside. Gutter overflow often appears as dark streaks on the wall just below the roof edge, and as wet drywall in the room behind it.

Stains that appear in the days after the storm can still be storm damage. Wall cavities hold water and release it slowly.

Storm Safety Right Now, While Water Is Coming In

Storm safety in the first hour

Storms bring hazards beyond water. Downed lines, broken glass and unstable limbs are all dangerous. Stay inside until the weather passes.

  • If a power line is down or sparking, stay away and call 911 and your electric utility.
  • If water touches outlets or the electrical panel, stay out. Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside. The Washington Gas leak line is 844-WASHGAS, option 1.
  • Do not wade through basement water. Floating objects and live circuits are not visible.
  • Take photos of damage before cleanup, including exterior damage if it is safe to look.

Hyattsville Storm History and Drainage

The city's own stormwater study, the Lower Ward 1 Resilient Stormwater Systems Study, says 41st Street experiences standing water during and after intense rain. It also notes that frequent downpours in 2018, the wettest year on record for the Washington, DC area, spurred multiple flood events.

The study used two storms to calibrate its model: August 2014, with 2.96 inches over about 5.5 hours, and July 2017, with 4.21 inches over about 15 hours. It says much of the storm drain system dates from 1960 to 1990. Older drains and inlets can clog or overload, so a basement near a low street can take water even if no river overflows.

The Prince George's County flood history page lists Tropical Storm Agnes in June 1972 as the county's storm of record, with Green Meadows in Hyattsville among the hardest hit areas. It also lists Hurricane Isabel in September 2003, with wind damage, outages and localized flooding.

Storm Claims and Your Policy

Insurance after a storm

The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies do not cover flooding, and a standard homeowners policy almost never covers flood damage. Wind-driven rain, plumbing and flood are often treated differently, so check your policy and ask your insurer how your loss is classified.

Its After the Storm guide advises reporting damage to your producer or insurer as soon as possible, keeping a record of everyone you talk with, and being present when the adjuster visits to point out all damage. It also says to compare multiple contractor bids with the adjuster's report before settling.

Before the Next Storm

A few chores reduce the water that gets inside.

Prevention and mistakes

  • ✓Clear gutters and extend downspouts away from the foundation.
  • ✓Test the sump pump and check the discharge hose before heavy rain, and confirm it does not point at a neighbor's property.
  • ✓Check caulk and weatherstripping around windows and doors.
  • ✓Do not leave wet carpet or boxes in place. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.
  • ✓Check the FEMA flood map for your address at msc.fema.gov, and remember a flood policy through the NFIP generally has a 30-day waiting period.
