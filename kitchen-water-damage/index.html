What Kitchen Water Damage Means

Kitchen water damage restoration is the removal, drying and repair of materials soaked by water in the room where plumbing, appliances and cabinetry sit closest together. A small sink leak can wet a cabinet floor, run under the cabinet base and reach the subfloor within hours. The 24/7 line at (301) 936-0619 takes these calls, and same-day response is offered when available.

This page covers the kitchen as a room. If the water came from a dishwasher, refrigerator line or washer, the cause is covered on the appliance leak water damage page. Here the focus is what the water did to the cabinets, floor and walls.

Where Water Travels in a Kitchen

Kitchens hide water better than most rooms, because the cabinets sit on top of the places that stay wet.

Under the sink

Supply lines, shutoff valves, the drain trap and the garbage disposal all sit in a small cabinet. A slow drip often shows up first as a swollen cabinet floor.

Behind the toe kick

The toe kick is the recessed panel at the base of the cabinets. Water collects behind it and wets the cabinet bottoms and subfloor out of sight.

Under the cabinet base

Cabinets are often installed before flooring in many kitchens, so water can run under the whole run of cabinets and across the subfloor beneath them.

Flooring transitions

Water follows seams, thresholds and the edge where tile or vinyl meets hardwood or carpet. Wet trim and wall bases show where it traveled.

Walls and ceilings below

A leak on a second-floor kitchen drips into the ceiling below. A leaking supply line inside a wall wets drywall behind the sink.

First Safety Steps in a Wet Kitchen

Kitchens mix water, power and gas

Shut the fixture valve under the sink, or close the main valve if the water keeps running. Outlets for the dishwasher, disposal and microwave sit near water, so cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.

  • If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 and your electric utility before the line.
  • If you smell gas near a range, leave the building and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside. The leak line is 844-WASHGAS, option 1.
  • Move food, small appliances and valuables up onto dry counters. Do not pull a heavy refrigerator through standing water.
  • Do not open or lift swollen cabinet panels yet. They can crumble and hide wet insulation.

How Wet Cabinets and Floors Get Dried

  1. 1

    Find the wet areas

    A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera show water in the cabinet bases, subfloor and walls before anything is cut.

  2. 2

    Open access

    Toe kicks come off, and small holes or panels are opened so air and drying equipment reach the cabinet cavity.

  3. 3

    Extract and dry

    Wet vacuums remove the standing water. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, sometimes with drying mats under floors, dry the room.

  4. 4

    Remove what cannot recover

    Swollen cabinet bases, soaked pad or flooring and wet drywall are removed. Readings guide each decision.

  5. 5

    Dry check and handoff

    Daily readings confirm dry framing and subfloor before a repair crew rebuilds. See water damage repair for the rebuild stage.

Does Insurance Cover a Kitchen Leak?

Sudden or slow

The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies will not cover damage from a slow leak, and many will not pay to repair or replace the pipe or drain itself. A sudden break under the sink is treated differently from a drip that went on for months.

Check your policy, photograph the cabinets and floor before anything is removed, and ask your insurer what applies. Do not assume a claim will be approved.

Mistakes That Ruin Kitchen Cabinets

  • ✓Mopping and moving on. Water under cabinets stays wet and wicks into the subfloor.
  • ✓Running a space heater at the cabinets. Fast heat can warp panels and dries the surface only.
  • ✓Re-installing a toe kick over a wet cavity. It traps moisture where it can grow mold. The EPA advises drying within 24 to 48 hours, and see mold remediation if the cavity smells musty.
  • ✓Replacing the floor before the subfloor reads dry.
  • ✓Skipping the plumber. Drying does not fix a leaking trap or valve.
