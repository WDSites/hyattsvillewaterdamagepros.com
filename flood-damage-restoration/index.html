First Steps When Floodwater Enters Your Hyattsville Home

Floodwater in the house is an emergency, and the (301) 936-0619 line answers 24/7. Flood damage restoration is the extraction, cleaning, drying and rebuild work after storm runoff, a swollen creek or groundwater gets into a building. Crews respond the same day when available, including nights and weekends.

While you wait, get everyone out of the water and keep children and pets away from it. Stop any water you control, such as a running fixture, and shut the main water valve if a supply line is involved. Lift valuables and papers to a higher shelf or floor.

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. If water is at outlet height, stay out and call 911 and your electric utility first.

Raw Sewage Coming Up the Floor Drain or Toilet

If sewage is rising in a basement floor drain or backing up a toilet, stop using the plumbing and stay out of the wet area. This water is grossly contaminated, and cleaning it up is flood damage restoration work, not a mop-and-bucket job. Call the line, then read the sewage section below to see who handles the sewer pipe itself.

Stay Out: Electric, Gas and Contaminated Water

Do this before anything else

Water around an electrical panel, outlets or plugged-in appliances is a shock hazard. Stay out, cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water, and call 911 and your electric utility before you call the line.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Washington Gas (844-WASHGAS, option 1) from outside.
  • The CDC says floodwater can contain dangerous bacteria from overflowing sewage. Do not wade through it, and keep children and pets out.
  • The CDC also warns never to use bleach in a closed space.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

Most flooding in a Hyattsville home ends up in the basement or the lowest level, because that is where runoff and groundwater settle. Water that came in from outside is usually Category 2 or 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard. Carpet, pad and the lower part of drywall are often removed instead of dried.

The work runs in order. Pumps take the standing water out, extraction units pull the rest from floors and low spots, and crews cut out wet porous materials. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the slab and framing, and moisture meters confirm the readings before walls close. The pumping stage matches emergency water removal, with added cleaning for contaminated water.

Check what sits low in the room. Water heaters, furnaces and panels in a flooded basement should be inspected by a qualified technician before they run again. The city's resident brochure says sump pump discharge must not go onto a neighboring property or over the public right of way.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

A sewage backup is Category 3 water. Cleanup means removing contaminated porous materials, cleaning hard surfaces with detergent and water, and drying the structure.

Who is responsible for the sewer line depends. WSSC Water says the homeowner maintains the line from the property line to the building, and WSSC maintains its main. WSSC tells customers to call a WSSC-registered plumber first. If the blockage is in WSSC's section, WSSC clears it at no charge, but the homeowner pays the plumber first.

WSSC's 24-hour emergency line, 301-206-4002, takes reports of a sewer backup into the home. WSSC also has a claims office that handles possible reimbursement for property damage. Only a registered WSSC master plumber may dig or repair underground sewer pipes, with a WSSC permit and inspection.

Plumber and WSSC deal with the pipe, and the restoration line deals with the mess. Until crews have cleaned the room, do not run sinks, showers or washers that drain to the same line.

What the City's Own Records Say About Flooding

The city's Lower Ward 1 Resilient Stormwater Systems Study, run from 2018 to 2020, set out to find improvements to manage flood risks. It says 41st Street experiences standing water during and after intense rain. A basement near a low street or a clogged inlet can take on water in a downpour even when no river overflows.

The study calibrated its model with two storms: August 2014, with 2.96 inches over about 5.5 hours, and July 2017, with 4.21 inches over about 15 hours. It also says much of the storm drain system dates from 1960 to 1990.

The study notes a Hyattsville-Riverdale levee system that protects some areas from the 100-year flood, so neighbors in Riverdale Park share part of that setting. Drains and sewers can still back up behind a levee.

The county's flood history page lists June 1972 Tropical Storm Agnes as the county's storm of record, with the Green Meadows subdivision in Hyattsville among the hardest hit. It also lists Hurricane Isabel in September 2003, with localized flooding. To see if your address is in a mapped flood zone, search it at msc.fema.gov.

What Restoration Does and Does Not Do

Two honest limits

Restoration is not waterproofing. Crews remove water and damaged materials and dry the structure, but they do not seal a foundation or fix grading, drains or a failed sump pump. If water keeps coming in, the cause needs its own repair.

Crawl space and whole-house flooding is handled here too. The same extraction and drying steps apply, adjusted for access and for the number of rooms involved.

From Extraction to Rebuild

  1. 1

    Extract and assess

    Pumps and extraction units remove standing water. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map what is still wet.

  2. 2

    Remove and clean

    Wet porous materials come out, and hard surfaces are cleaned. Once the structure is dry, water damage cleanup covers contents, surfaces and finishing.

  3. 3

    Dry and verify

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry standards. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and if mold is already present, mold remediation is a separate job.

  4. 4

    Rebuild

    Drywall, flooring and trim are replaced. Permit rules vary by the work, so ask Prince George's DPIE or the city before rebuilding walls, electrical or plumbing.

Flood Insurance and Sewer Backup Coverage

General guidance, not a coverage decision

The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies do not cover flooding. Flood coverage is a separate policy, and FEMA says an NFIP flood policy generally has a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect.

Sewer or water backup is usually an optional endorsement that insurers must offer. Many policies will not cover the cost to repair or replace the pipe or drain itself. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies, and take dated photos before cleanup starts.
