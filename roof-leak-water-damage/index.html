How Roof Leak Water Damage Starts

Roof leak water damage is the harm done to insulation, ceilings, walls and framing by water that gets past the roof covering. The roofer fixes the opening. The restoration crew handles the wet building materials left under it.

Call (301) 936-0619 if water is coming through now. The line runs 24/7, and crews respond the same day when available. Meanwhile, put a bucket or tub under drips and move furniture away from the wet spot.

Roofs fail where water changes direction: at flashing around chimneys and vents, along valleys, under lifted shingles and at skylights. A small gap can pass water for weeks before anyone looks up.

What the Stain on Your Ceiling Is Telling You

A brown ring on a ceiling is rarely where the roof leaks. Water travels along rafters, down a wall or across a ceiling board before it finds a place to show. The stain can sit several feet from the entry point.

By the time you see color, the insulation above is usually wet and heavy. Wet insulation loses its ability to slow heat and can hold water against the drywall for days.

A bulge or sag in the ceiling means water is pooled on top of the board. Stay out from under it, because the water can come down all at once.

Roof Leak Safety Steps for the First Hour

Roof leak safety before anything else

Do not climb onto a wet or icy roof to look for the hole. Falls are the real danger. Water above a light fixture or ceiling fan can also carry electricity down into the room.

  • If water drips from a light fixture or you see water around outlets, leave the light off and stay out. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 and your electric utility before you call the line.
  • A sagging ceiling can fall without warning, so stay out from under it and keep children and pets out of the room.
  • Move furniture, rugs and electronics, and cover what cannot move with plastic.
  • Take photos of the ceiling, the attic if it is safe to enter, and the roof from the ground.

Where Water Gets In

Each entry point leaves a different pattern inside the house.

Flashing at chimneys and vents

Metal flashing seals the joint where the roof meets a chimney or pipe. When sealant ages or the metal lifts, water runs down the masonry or pipe into the ceiling below.

Shingles that lift or crack

Wind can lift shingles and age can crack them. Water gets under and soaks the roof deck, then drips through the attic floor.

Ice dams at the eaves

Snow melts on a warm roof and refreezes at the cold edge. The ice ridge holds water back until it backs up under shingles and runs into the wall below.

Valleys and skylights

Valleys collect water from two roof planes at once. A small defect there sends a lot of water into one spot.

Gutters that back up

Clogged gutters let water spill behind the fascia and into the soffit, leaving stains near the top of exterior walls.

Roof Leaks and Your Homeowners Policy

What the Maryland Insurance Administration says about roofs

The Maryland Insurance Administration says policies generally cover sudden, accidental losses from perils such as wind and hail, but not maintenance. It also says damage from wear and tear or deterioration of a roof over time generally will not be paid for.

Some policies carry a separate wind, hail or hurricane deductible. Read the loss settlement section and ask your insurer what applies. The state also warns about unsolicited roof inspection offers, since some contractors misrepresent wear and tear as storm damage.

Mistakes and Prevention

Roof leaks reward quick action and punish guessing.

Do this, not that

  • ✓Do not ignore a stain that grows after each rain. Mark its edge with a pencil and note the date.
  • ✓Do not cover a wet ceiling with paint. Moisture stays trapped, and mold needs only water. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours.
  • ✓Do not pay a roofer who offers to waive your deductible. The Maryland Insurance Administration says state law makes it illegal for contractors or roofers to offer to waive a deductible.
  • ✓Do clear gutters and check flashing in the fall, when leaves clog drains and the weather is still safe for a look.
  • ✓If the leak followed a big storm, storm water damage explains what else wind and rain may have reached.

After the Roof Is Sealed

Once the roof is repaired and the building is dry, water damage repair replaces insulation, drywall and paint. If the leak went on for weeks, a mold check belongs in the plan, and mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520 when mold is found.
