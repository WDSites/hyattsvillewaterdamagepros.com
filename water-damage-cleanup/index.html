Cleanup begins once the water is stopped

Wet carpet, soggy drywall and a floor covered in debris need action today. Call (301) 936-0619: the emergency line runs 24/7, and the first call starts with questions about where the water came from and what it touched. Water damage cleanup in Hyattsville, MD is the removal of water, debris and contaminated materials, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and deodorizing the space.

Before the crew arrives, stop the source, keep children and pets out of the affected rooms and avoid wading through water you cannot identify. Lift valuables, photograph the damage and turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. The water mitigation page covers the drying side of the job.

Cleanup and drying overlap. Wet materials come out first, which makes drying faster and keeps odor from building.

The first 48 hours after a leak or flood

Time matters because wet porous materials hold moisture and carry smell. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and cleanup is what makes that target reachable. Soaked carpet pad, wet insulation and swollen drywall slow drying down if they stay in place.

Same-day response is available when crews are free, and a night or weekend call is handled the same way. Cleanup that waits a week costs more, because stained surfaces, odors and hidden moisture all grow with time.

Water categories in plain words

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration groups water into three categories by how contaminated it is. The category decides what can be cleaned and what must be thrown away.

Water categories under the S500 system, in plain language
CategoryPlain meaningTypical sourcesHow cleanup changes
Category 1Clean source waterA broken supply line, an overflowing sink with clean water, a tub overflowMost materials can be dried and kept if the work starts fast.
Category 2Significantly contaminatedWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine but no fecesCarpet and pad usually come out. Hard surfaces get cleaned and sanitized.
Category 3Grossly contaminatedSewage, rising floodwater, water that sat long enough to turnPorous materials come out, the area is sanitized and crews wear protective gear.

Clean water can become Category 2 or 3 if it sits. A supply line leak left for days is no longer a clean-water job.

What comes out and what can stay

A cleanup crew sorts every material into keep or remove. These are the usual calls, and a written scope should show them room by room.

Carpet

Carpet from a clean-water loss can often be cleaned and dried in place if it is caught early. Carpet touched by Category 2 or 3 water is usually discarded.

Carpet pad

Pad acts like a sponge and is replaced in most cases, even when the carpet above can stay.

Drywall

Drywall soaked through or touched by contaminated water is cut out. Wall board that stayed above the wet line may dry in place, and meters confirm it.

Insulation

Wet fiberglass or cellulose is removed so the wall cavity can dry. Dry insulation higher in the cavity can often be left.

Contents

Hard goods, such as metal, plastic and sealed wood, can usually be cleaned. Soft goods like upholstery, mattresses and stuffed items from contaminated water are generally discarded.

Cleaning, sanitizing and odor control

After wet materials are removed, surfaces are cleaned, then sanitized with an antimicrobial product matched to the water category. Cleaning removes soil that sanitizer cannot reach through, so the order matters. HEPA vacuums pick up fine debris, and extraction units pull residue from hard floors.

Odor control follows the source. Smell comes from damp materials and from bacteria growing in them, so removing the material and drying the space does most of the work. Air scrubbers and deodorizing products help in what is left, but they do not replace removal. A musty room that smells again after deodorizing usually still has moisture inside a wall or under a floor.

If cleanup turns up visible growth, treat that as a separate question. Mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520, and the EPA's do-it-yourself guideline applies only to areas under about 10 square feet.

Sewage, floodwater and bleach: safety first

Do not wade in contaminated water or mix cleaners

The CDC says floodwater can contain dangerous bacteria from overflowing sewage. Keep children, pets and anyone with open cuts out of it, and wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter.

The CDC also says never to use bleach in a closed space and never to mix bleach with ammonia or other cleansers. Open windows, wear gloves and keep products separate. The EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine practice for mold cleanup.

For a sewer backup into the home, WSSC Water's 24-hour line is 301-206-4002. If water touches electrical outlets or you smell gas, leave and call 911 and your utility before calling the line.

Contents handling and your insurance claim

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while a gradual leak often is not. Flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood coverage, and sewer backup often needs an endorsement. Check your policy or ask your insurer what it requires.

Document before anything is thrown away

  • ✓Take wide and close photos of every room and the water source before cleanup starts.
  • ✓List damaged items with a description, age and where you bought them. Photograph each one.
  • ✓Keep a small sample of removed carpet, pad or flooring if it is safe to handle and your insurer asks for it. Bag it and label it.
  • ✓Save receipts for emergency costs, such as a hotel, disposal or temporary repairs.
  • ✓Write down who you spoke to and when. Maryland's claim settlement rule, COMAR 31.15.07.03, treats failing to acknowledge a notice within 15 working days as an unfair practice, with exceptions.
  • ✓Ask the crew for their written scope and moisture records at the end of the job.
