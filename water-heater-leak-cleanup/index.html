Water Heater Leaking Right Now

Water heater leak cleanup is the removal of spilled water and the drying of every surface it reached after a tank, valve or fitting fails. If your heater is leaking right now, the (301) 936-0619 line answers 24/7, and crews respond the same day when available. No arrival time is promised on the call.

While you wait, close the cold water supply valve on the pipe feeding the heater. If the valve is stuck or underwater, shut the main water valve for the house instead. Lift boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor, and keep children and pets away from the puddle, because water from the tank can be hot.

A heater rarely leaks in a place that is easy to see. Many sit in basements, utility closets and corners behind a furnace door, so water often runs under walls and flooring before anyone notices. By then the job is no longer a mop-up. It is a drying job.

Water Heater Safety Before Anything Else

Gas, power and hot water come first

A heater can be a gas appliance or an electric one, and each has its own danger. Decide which you have before you touch anything, then follow the matching rule below.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside. The Washington Gas leak line is 844-WASHGAS, option 1. Do not flip switches or use a lighter on the way out.
  • For an electric heater, turn off its breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If water is touching the panel, outlets or the heater wiring, stay out and call 911 and your electric utility before you call the line.
  • Do not reach into hot standing water or touch a hot relief valve pipe. Water that has been inside the tank can scald.
  • Do not relight a gas pilot or restart a unit that has been wet. A licensed plumber should check it first.

Why Tanks Leak and Valves Discharge

Most water heater floods trace back to one of a few causes. Knowing which one you have helps the plumber and helps when you talk to your insurer.

Tank corrosion

Steel tanks rust from the inside over time, and the leak often starts at a seam or the bottom. A tank that has split cannot be repaired. It has to be replaced.

Sediment and age

Minerals settle at the bottom of the tank and bake into a crust. The crust traps heat, stresses the steel and can speed up failure. An old tank that was never flushed is the usual suspect.

Pressure relief valve discharge

The temperature and pressure relief valve opens to let out water or steam when pressure builds. A pipe usually carries that water to a drain or to the floor. A valve that drips often points to high pressure or a worn valve.

Loose fittings and drain valve

Supply connections, the drain valve at the base and the anode rod port can all weep. These are slower leaks, and they can soak a subfloor for weeks.

The city says the Hyattsville Historic District holds nearly 1,000 buildings, and the National Register entry says the town grew mainly after the 1870s. Houses of that age often have heaters tucked into basements with old floor drains, mixed pipe materials and brick or block walls that hold moisture. A leak in one of those rooms can wick into wall bases and stair stringers.

Apartment buildings add another twist. The Census Bureau's QuickFacts puts the owner-occupied rate in Hyattsville at 48.4 percent for 2020 to 2024, so about half of households rent. Many renters share a utility closet or sit above one, and water from a tank can reach the unit below. If the heater is not in your unit, tell your landlord right away and call the line for the wet areas you can reach.

Replacing the heater is a separate job from the cleanup. Permit rules vary by the work, so ask Prince George's DPIE or the city before you replace a tank or touch gas or electrical lines.

How the Cleanup Works

  1. 1

    The call

    The dispatcher asks what kind of heater it is, where it sits and how far the water has run.

  2. 2

    Extraction

    Crews pull standing water with wet vacuums and portable extraction units, then lift soaked carpet and pad if present.

  3. 3

    Moisture mapping

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled into drywall, baseboards and subfloor.

  4. 4

    Drying

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference points. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published guide for this work, so ask any company whether its crews follow it.

Water Heater Leaks and Your Policy

The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies will not cover damage that results from a slow leak. A tank that bursts in one event is a different story from a valve that wept for months.

The difference often matters. The MIA also says many policies will not pay to repair or replace the pipe or drain itself, and a failed tank may be treated the same way. Check your policy for how it handles the unit and the damage.

Timing matters too. Maryland's unfair claim settlement rule, COMAR 31.15.07.03, lists failing to acknowledge a claim notice within 15 working days as an unfair practice, with exceptions. Report the loss promptly and take photos before cleanup.

Keep the wet materials the crew removes until you are told you can discard them. Renters should know that a renters policy covers belongings, not the building, so the landlord's coverage handles the structure.

Mistakes to Avoid and Prevention

A few small habits cut the odds of a repeat and make a leak easier to catch.

  • ✓Do not plug or cap a relief valve discharge pipe. The valve is a safety device, and it needs a clear path to release pressure.
  • ✓Do not leave wet drywall behind a heater closet. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.
  • ✓Look at the base of the tank and the floor around it every few months, and put a drain pan with a leak sensor under heaters that sit above finished space.
  • ✓Ask a licensed plumber about flushing the tank and checking the relief valve, especially on an older unit.
  • ✓Know where the cold supply valve is before you need it, and clear the path to it.
