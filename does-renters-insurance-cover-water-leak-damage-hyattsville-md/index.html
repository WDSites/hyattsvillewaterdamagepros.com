Which policy covers what after a leak

A renters policy generally covers your belongings, such as furniture, clothing and electronics, when the water damage is sudden and accidental. The landlord's policy covers the building itself, including walls, floors and ceilings. The Maryland Insurance Administration's water damage handout draws the same line and says renters policies cover your belongings, not the building.

That split settles most arguments. If a pipe bursts above your unit and soaks your couch, the couch is a renters claim and the wet drywall is the landlord's job. Gradual leaks and neglect are often excluded, so check your policy and ask your insurer before you assume anything is paid.

The first hour when water comes through your ceiling

  1. 1

    Protect people and power

    If water is near an outlet, a light fixture or the panel, stay out of that room. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 or your electric utility for any electrical risk.

  2. 2

    Get the source stopped

    Knock on the unit above or call the landlord's emergency maintenance number. A leaking supply line can often be isolated at a shutoff valve, so ask them to close it.

  3. 3

    Lift what you can

    Move electronics, papers and soft goods onto dry furniture. A bulging ceiling can release a lot of water at once, so keep clear of it.

  4. 4

    Photograph before cleanup

    Take wide shots and close-ups of the ceiling, the floor and each damaged item before anything is moved or thrown out.

A paper trail both an adjuster and a landlord can use

Records made on the day of the leak carry more weight than memories a month later.

  • ✓Take dated photos and a slow video of every wet room, including the ceiling above and the floor below.
  • ✓List damaged belongings with brand, approximate age and where you bought them, and keep receipts if you have them.
  • ✓Hold on to wet items until your insurer says you may discard them, unless they are unsafe to keep.
  • ✓Write down times: when you noticed the leak, when you called maintenance, and who answered.
  • ✓Save every text and email with the landlord or property manager in one folder.
  • ✓Keep invoices for any drying, cleanup or temporary lodging you pay for.

Hyattsville, MD: what to know locally

Census QuickFacts puts Hyattsville's owner-occupied rate at 48.4 percent for 2020 to 2024, so roughly half of households rent. That makes the tenant's side of a leak a routine question here, not an odd one.

The Maryland Insurance Administration's claim-practices rule, COMAR 31.15.07.03, lists failing to acknowledge a claim notice within 15 working days as an unfair practice, with exceptions. It also covers affirming or denying coverage within 15 working days after completed forms arrive. Ask your insurer's adjuster which dates apply to your claim.

Putting the problem in writing to your landlord

A phone call leaves no record, so follow every call with an email or letter. Give the date, your unit number, what you see, where you think the water is coming from, and a request that the source be stopped and the unit inspected. Ask when repairs will start.

Send the message to the property manager and keep a copy. If the leak came from the unit above, say so plainly but stick to what you observed. Your lease and Maryland tenant rules decide the rest, so a tenant advocate or attorney can answer questions this article cannot.

What a restoration line can and cannot do for a tenant

Calling the line gets a crew dispatched for emergency water damage cleanup: extraction units to pull standing water, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry, and moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find wet material you cannot see. Same-day response is offered when available, at any hour.

In a rental, the owner usually has to approve work on the building, so a tenant may need the landlord's permission. The line cannot decide coverage, promise a claim will be paid, or override your lease. If drying has to begin before anyone agrees on repairs, water mitigation limits the spread while the paperwork catches up.
