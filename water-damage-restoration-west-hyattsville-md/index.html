Where West Hyattsville sits and how much of it is in the city

Water damage restoration in West Hyattsville covers a part of town that does not follow one clean line. The West Hyattsville Metro station is at 2700 Hamilton Street, and a public reference entry for the station places it west of the Ager Road and Queens Chapel Road intersection (Maryland Route 500). The station opened on December 11, 1993.

West Hyattsville is only partly inside the City of Hyattsville. A local news description of the city's wards says one ward is the part of West Hyattsville west of Queens Chapel and east of Ager Road, and another includes the area near the Metro station. County planning documents also describe unincorporated areas here, so a Hyattsville mailing address alone does not show which side of the city line you are on.

We do not claim a boundary here. The line covers the area around the station, and the dispatcher only needs a street address and a nearby landmark. If you are unsure whether you are in the city, that matters for permits and city services, but it does not change who picks up the phone.

What the county sector plan says about growth near the station

Prince George's County planning documents name a West Hyattsville-Queens Chapel Sector Plan, a resolution (CR-137-2025) that went on the County Council agenda for November 10, 2025. The county describes the plan as directing growth near the West Hyattsville transit center. The study area takes in parts of Mount Rainier, Hyattsville and Brentwood, plus unincorporated land.

The plan's edges are described as the Prince George's Plaza transit district to the northeast, Eastern Avenue to the southwest, Arundel Road to the southeast and the Northwest Branch of the Anacostia to the northwest. For a reader, the practical point is that more homes near the station means more shared walls, shared risers and shared ceilings. A leak that crosses units is the one that needs fast action.

A Hyattsville local news site has also covered proposals for townhomes and apartments on open parcels near Queens Chapel Road and Ager Road. Proposals are not finished buildings, so this page makes no claim about any specific building or its plumbing.

Upstairs-unit leaks and shared walls in Metro-area buildings

How water travels in a stacked building

This is general guidance for apartments and townhomes, not a statement about any particular building. Water from a washing machine hose, a toilet supply line or a dishwasher in an upper unit usually follows the pipe chase or the floor joists down. It shows up as a stain or drip on the ceiling below, often far from the real source.

Shared walls add a second path. Water can wick into the drywall at the base of a party wall and move sideways into the next unit. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera find that hidden water faster than tapping and guessing. Our guide to appliance leak water damage covers where water goes under and behind machines.

Townhomes near Queens Chapel Road

Townhomes share walls on both sides, so a leak in one can reach a neighbor even when the first owner sees little. Tell your neighbor and your homeowners association or property manager the day you find water. Early notice shortens the drying job and keeps arguments about timing to a minimum.

Water safety before anyone arrives

Do these things first

Stay out of standing water if outlets, power strips or the panel are wet or nearby. Turn off the water at the shutoff only if it is safe and reachable, and cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If not, call your electric utility or 911.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas company from outside.
  • If water came up through a toilet or floor drain, treat it as sewage. WSSC Water keeps a 24-hour line for sewer backups at 301-206-4002.
  • Do not use household fans or a household vacuum on sewage or other contaminated water. They can spread contamination.
  • Keep children and pets out of wet areas and lift belongings off the floor.

How extraction and drying work after a leak

  1. 1

    Find the source and stop it

    The crew confirms the leak is stopped or tells you which licensed plumber to call for the repair.

  2. 2

    Extract standing water

    Extraction units pull water from floors and carpet. This is the job of emergency water removal, and it comes before drying starts.

  3. 3

    Map the moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show wet drywall, insulation and subfloor, including areas behind shared walls.

  4. 4

    Dry with air movers and dehumidifiers

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for days while readings are checked. This is the core of water mitigation, and ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard to ask any company about.

  5. 5

    Rebuild once readings are dry

    Drywall, flooring and trim come after drying, as described under water damage repair.

Three mistakes in shared buildings

The first mistake is waiting to see if the stain dries by itself. A dry-looking ceiling can still have wet insulation above it. The second is repainting over a stain before anyone has checked the cavity with a moisture meter.

The third is leaving mold questions until later. Mold needs moisture, so the water source comes first, and mold remediation follows S520 if growth is found. Health questions belong with a physician. For the full service list, start at the home page.
