Two quotes for the same crawl space can look nothing alike. The gap usually lives in the scope, so learn to read the scope.

Why this page prints no dollar figures

Crawl space mold removal cost depends on six things. They are how much area is affected, how hard it is to reach, and where the moisture comes from. The rest are which materials must be removed, how much containment the job needs, and how clearance is confirmed afterward. Change any one and the price moves.

That is why no honest page can give you a number without seeing your space. Any figure printed on a web page ignores your access, your moisture source and your materials. We would rather show you how a scope is built so you can compare quotes on equal terms.

In Hyattsville, MD, a good quote should arrive as a written scope, with price explained before work starts. If a company names a total before anyone has looked underneath, ask what it assumed.

Why quotes for the same space differ

One company may price cleaning of framing surfaces only. Another may include removal of insulation and failed vapor material, a dehumidification plan and testing at the end. Both are honest bids, but they are not the same job.

Scope also changes with the finding. A crew that measures moisture readings in the framing and subfloor before it prices is quoting what it saw. A bid made from a phone photo is a guess that gets revised later.

Lowest and highest are rarely the right pick. Compare what each quote includes, what it leaves out and who is responsible if hidden damage appears when the insulation comes down. Ask each company to put exclusions in writing.

How a written scope gets built

  1. 1

    Inspect and measure

    A technician looks at framing, subfloor, insulation and ground cover, and takes moisture meter readings. Photos record what is found.

  2. 2

    Find the water

    Plumbing leaks, rain entry, poor drainage and condensation are checked. The scope names who fixes the source and in what order.

  3. 3

    List materials in and out

    Each material is marked as clean, treat or remove. Porous items such as insulation are listed separately from structural wood.

  4. 4

    Plan containment

    Barriers, negative air, filtered exhaust and entry rules are written down, so cost and process match.

  5. 5

    Set the finish line

    Dry readings, visual checks and clearance testing are named, along with who performs them.

Hyattsville, MD: local facts to bring to any quote

The state Home Improvement Commission's FAQ says the 2008 law that licensed mold remediators ended on July 1, 2019. With no license program running under that law today, ask each bidder what training and standards its crews follow. A named standard is a better answer than a logo.

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals, taken at Reagan National Airport about eight miles south, show 41.82 inches of precipitation a year and a July mean of 81.0 F. That is rain and warm summers to plan around, because outside water that reaches a low space can keep it damp.

The city's stormwater study found 41st Street holds standing water during and after intense rain, and it dates much of the drain system to 1960 through 1990. Whether your home has a crawl space at all is something your own inspection will show. If it does, check where roof and yard water goes, since drainage is a moisture source the scope should name.

Safety before anyone goes under the house

Look from the hatch first

A wet crawl space can hide standing water, exposed wiring and sewage. Look from the access hatch with a flashlight before you decide whether to go in.

  • Stay out if water is standing near wiring, outlets or a panel.
  • Stay out if the water smells like sewage or came from a drain.
  • Take photos from the hatch for your insurance file instead of crawling in.
  • Call the line and describe what you see before you disturb anything.

Moisture first, mold second

Per the EPA, mold will not grow without water or moisture. A crawl space cleaned without correcting the water source is a space waiting to grow again, so any mold remediation plan should name the drying and the fix. Where wet materials remain, water mitigation drying belongs in the same plan.

No honest contractor will say mold will never return. Health questions about exposure belong with a physician, and insurance questions belong with your insurer, since the state insurance regulator says most policies leave mold out unless a covered peril caused it.
