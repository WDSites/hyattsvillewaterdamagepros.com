A year of water trouble in Mount Rainier, season by season

Winter: freezing nights and old walls

The nearest long-term NOAA station, Reagan National Airport, shows a January average low of 30.1 F and about 13.70 inches of snow in a normal year. In Mount Rainier, 33.1 percent of homes were built in 1939 or earlier, and supply lines in outside walls of houses that old are where a hard freeze does its damage. If a pipe splits, close the valve first, then call.

Spring and summer: downpours and Eastern Avenue runoff

The same station records 41.82 inches of precipitation in a normal year. The city's project page for 31st Street and Perry Street says the work is expected to significantly reduce flooding and erosion by capturing a large amount of runoff from Eastern Avenue, which tells you where water has been collecting. City pages also list county-led green infrastructure work on Arundel Road between 31st and 34th Streets.

July to September: air conditioners

July averages 81.0 F at that station, so air conditioners run for weeks. A clogged condensate line drips quietly into a ceiling or closet, and a slow leak like that is a common source of mold, because mold needs moisture. If you see a ceiling stain under an upstairs unit, say so on the call.

When water is spreading through a Mount Rainier bungalow or walk-up

The city has about 1,100 detached houses and a large share of small apartment buildings, so water often crosses a ceiling into a neighbor's home. Close the source if you can reach it, move off wet floors and call (301) 936-0619. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day.

Tell the neighbors below you. A leak that starts in an upstairs bathroom can reach two or three units before anyone sees a stain.

Hold the damage down while you wait for the dispatcher

Valve, then fixture

Try the fixture shutoff under a sink or behind a toilet before the main. If neither works, close the main near the meter or where the line enters the house.

Lights and outlets

Leave switches alone in a room where water drips from a ceiling fixture. Shut the breakers for that area from a dry spot, or call your electric utility.

Buckets, plastic and lift

Catch drips in buckets, put plastic under furniture legs and lift curtains and rugs away from the wet spot.

Photograph, then call

Take photos or video of the damage, then call (301) 936-0619. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

ZIP 20712, Rhode Island Avenue and the rest of the city

Mount Rainier has one ZIP, 20712, the code on City Hall at 1 Municipal Place. The city covers 0.64 square miles and counted 8,333 residents in 2020, while the ZIP's population is 9,526 because it reaches past city limits. Dispatch for all of it works nights and weekends from our Hyattsville base, about 1.4 miles off.

Rhode Island Avenue (Route One) and the 34th Street shops are the commercial spines, and the Gateway Arts District follows two miles of that avenue. Upshur Park at 4229 34th Street, Frederick Richardson Memorial Park at 4511 31st Street and 30th Street Park at 4208 30th Street help a dispatcher place a call, so give your cross street.

Brentwood lies about half a mile east between center points, and Bladensburg about 2.1 miles east.

Bungalows, Sears houses and walk-ups: who lives behind the walls

The city says it was incorporated by charter on 14 April 1910, after a streetcar line to downtown Washington began running in early 1897. Its pages describe a collection of Sears houses and Craftsman-style homes, and the historic listing on the National Register came in 1990.

American Community Survey figures posted by the state planning department show 75.7 percent of occupied homes rented, with 35.4 percent in buildings of 5 to 9 units and 19.9 percent in buildings of 3 or 4 units. Homes from 1939 or earlier are 33.1 percent, or 1,281 units. In a small building with shared walls and old plumbing, one leak becomes several households' problem, so renters should photograph damage and tell the landlord in writing.

Street sewer work and backups

If sewage backs up while the street is torn up

WSSC Water's sewer rehabilitation project in Mount Rainier is underway through December 2026, according to the city. Its notice lists Varnum Street, Kaywood Drive, 28th Street, and 29th and Shepherd Street.

Work nearby does not prove the work caused a backup, and the answer depends on where the blockage sits. Keep out of the water, call WSSC's 24-hour line at 301-206-4002, and call (301) 936-0619 for cleanup and drying.

Four Mount Rainier problems, sized by how far the water got

Split pipe in a winter wall

Scope: one wall and the floor below

Emergency water removal clears the floor with extraction units, and moisture meters then show how far into the wall the water went.

Slow drip in a small apartment building

Scope: two or three units

Water mitigation uses a thermal imaging camera to follow the wet path, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry ceilings and walls across units.

A leak that sat for weeks

Scope: moisture found late

When growth is already visible, mold remediation uses containment and HEPA air scrubbers, with testing and cleanup following ANSI/IICRC S520.

Rebuild after drying

Scope: finishes and framing

Once readings are dry, water damage repair puts back drywall, floors and trim. Scope depends on the water category, area, materials and drying days.

City projects, permits and who to ask

Local facts that affect your repair

The city's website carries a building permit application and checklist and says certain permits and licenses are issued by Prince George's County. The exact split was not stated, so ask the city or Prince George's DPIE before any rebuild that touches walls, wiring or plumbing.

Upshur Park, at 4229 34th Street, is described by the city as an environmentally friendly park with Maryland native plantings, and the city lists the Nature Center as the county's only urban nature center. Neither affects your insurance, but both are handy landmarks for directions.

The map service at msc.fema.gov shows the effective panel for your address. The county flood history page does not name Mount Rainier, so the map is your best address-level check.
