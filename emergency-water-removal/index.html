Standing Water in Your Hyattsville Home Right Now

If water is pooling in your Hyattsville home right now, the next hour matters. The (301) 936-0619 line runs 24/7, and crews respond the same day when available. Emergency water removal is the fast pumping and extraction of standing water from floors and basements before it soaks into drywall, subfloors and framing.

While you wait, stop the source if that is safe. Shut the main water valve, which is often near the water heater or where the supply enters the house. Lift valuables, electronics and rugs onto dry furniture, and keep people and pets out of any water that might be contaminated.

Turn off power to wet areas only if the breaker panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Take photos before you move things.

Local housing makes speed matter. The city counts nearly 1,000 buildings in the Hyattsville Historic District, which the National Register entry says grew mainly after the 1870s. Buildings of that age tend to have older supply lines, drains and wall cavities, and wall cavities are where hidden water settles.

Stay Out If Water Meets Power, Gas or Sewage

Safety comes before the phone call

Water around an electrical panel, outlets or plugged-in appliances is a shock hazard. Stay out of the room. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 and your electric utility before you call the line.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside. The Washington Gas leak line is 844-WASHGAS, option 1.
  • If the water came up through a drain or toilet, treat it as sewage. Stay out, keep children and pets away, and call WSSC Water at 301-206-4002 for a sewer backup into the home.
  • Do not wade through deep basement water. Hidden steps, floating objects and live circuits are hazards you cannot see.

Burst Pipe Water Damage

A burst pipe keeps pouring until someone closes a valve, so the shutoff is the first job. Close the main shutoff valve inside your home, then open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain what is left in the lines. Pipes inside your walls, under sinks and at the water heater are on your side of the meter.

If the water is coming from a main in the street instead of from inside your home, that is a WSSC Water problem. WSSC takes water main break reports 24 hours a day at 301-206-4002. Its website says main repairs typically take 4 to 6 hours after crews dig up and uncover the damaged pipe.

Cold snaps cause many burst pipes. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals from the nearest long-term station, Reagan National Airport, put the January mean at 37.5 F with an average low of 30.1 F. A typical January night in this area dips below freezing, so pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces and unheated basements are the usual trouble spots.

Once the pipe is off and the water is out, the wall cavity still holds moisture. Water mitigation is the drying stage that follows, using moisture meters, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry framing and subfloor before anything is closed up.

The Equipment That Pulls Standing Water Out

A crew usually brings several machines and switches as the water gets shallower.

Submersible pumps

A submersible pump sits in the water and pushes it through a discharge hose to a drain or outside. It clears the first several inches of a basement quickly.

Truck-mounted extraction units

A truck-mounted unit runs a powerful vacuum from the curb through long hoses into the house. It pulls water out of carpet, pad and low spots.

Portable extraction units

Portable extractors reach upper floors, finished rooms and tight stairs, where hoses cannot go.

Wet vacuums

Wet vacuums finish corners, stair treads and baseboard edges. They also handle small appliance leaks.

Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras

After extraction, a moisture meter reads wet drywall, wood and subfloor. A thermal imaging camera shows wet areas behind walls.

Insurance and Emergency Water Removal

General guidance, not a coverage decision

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by homeowners policies. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not. The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies do not cover flooding, and that sewer or water backup is an optional endorsement.

Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies to you. Take dated photos before cleanup starts and keep damaged items until you are told otherwise. Renters policies cover your belongings, not the building.

After the Water Is Out

Removal is only the first stage. If the water came from a storm or rising groundwater, flood damage restoration covers the contaminated-water cleanup, material removal and rebuild planning. For clean-water losses, water damage cleanup handles surfaces, contents and finishing work after the structure is dry.

Heavy rain is a real basement risk here. The city's own Lower Ward 1 stormwater study says much of the storm drain system dates from 1960 to 1990, and that 41st Street experiences standing water during and after intense rain. A basement near a low street or a clogged inlet can flood in a downpour even when no river overflows.
