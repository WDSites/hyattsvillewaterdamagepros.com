A Hyattsville community development corporation welcome guide from 2016 says University Hills lies just north of Prince George's Plaza. It describes the housing as single-family ranches and colonial-style homes, and it names a neighborhood park known as Duck Pond Park. That is a description of the area, not a survey of every house.

The University Hills Area Civic Association publishes newsletters, and those newsletters say it covers the former University Hills and Ashland subdivisions plus Rosemary Terrace. They mention streets such as Stanford, Purdue, Rutgers and Pony Trail, and St. Mark the Evangelist church and school on Adelphi Road. The newsletters do not set formal boundaries, so we make no boundary claim.

That report covers that area only. If you are unsure whether your street is inside the city, ask the city or Prince George's DPIE before a rebuild, because the city's resident brochure says most construction work needs a DPIE permit.

Single-Family Houses and the Leaks They Get

The welcome guide describes houses, not apartment blocks. That points to sources found inside a house. No local source counts how often any of these happen in University Hills, so treat them as realistic, not as statistics.

Water heaters

A failed tank or a relief valve that discharges can cover a utility room or closet floor quickly. A water heater leak is handled by shutting off the cold supply only if the valve is dry and reachable. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside.

Washers, dishwashers and ice makers

Supply hoses and valves leak behind and under the machine, and water can reach the subfloor before anyone sees it. A damp cabinet base or a soft spot at the floor edge is a common sign. Moisture meters trace the path in appliance leak water damage jobs.

Roof and storm intrusion

A roof leak wets attic insulation first and shows later as a ceiling stain. Wind-driven rain can also enter at windows and doors, and a full gutter can push water down a wall. Crews handle these under roof leak water damage and storm water damage work.

Safety Steps Before Anyone Touches the Water

Safety in the first hour

Water and electricity do not mix, and sewage carries bacteria. Take these steps in order.

  • Do not enter standing water if outlets, cords or the electrical panel may be wet. Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • Shut off the water supply to the leaking fixture or the main valve, only if it is safe and reachable.
  • Do not run household fans or a household vacuum on sewage or other contaminated water.
  • For a sewer backup into the home, WSSC Water's 24-hour emergency line is 301-206-4002.
  • For gas smell, sparking, or any immediate danger, leave and call 911.

How Extraction and Drying Work

  1. 1

    Find the source and the wet area

    Crews stop or confirm the stopped source. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meters show wet drywall, insulation and subfloor behind the surface.

  2. 2

    Extract standing water

    Pumps and extraction units pull water from floors and carpet. Removing it early limits how far it wicks into walls.

  3. 3

    Dry the structure

    Air movers push air across wet surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

  4. 4

    Monitor and document

    Readings are logged until materials reach dry standards. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for this work, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it.

Backup Coverage, Flood Coverage and Your Policy

What the Maryland Insurance Administration says

The Maryland Insurance Administration says homeowners policies do not cover all water damage. Flooding is typically excluded and comes through the National Flood Insurance Program. Sewer, drain or sump backup usually needs an endorsement, and Maryland law requires insurers to offer it. A 2019 bulletin from the agency says the offer must be made in writing at application and renewal.

The agency also says foundation seepage is generally not covered. Coverage depends on the cause of the loss and your policy terms, and no one can promise approval. Check your policy for a backup endorsement, or ask your insurer.

Mistakes That Make a Leak Cost More

Common errors in house leaks

  • ✓Waiting to see if a damp wall dries by itself. Wet drywall and insulation can hold water for days.
  • ✓Using bleach in a closed space or mixing it with ammonia or other cleansers.
  • ✓Assuming a backup is the city's job. WSSC Water says the owner maintains the sewer line from the property line to the building.
  • ✓Hiring anyone but a WSSC-registered master plumber to dig or repair an underground sewer pipe.
  • ✓Tearing out materials before photos and moisture readings are taken.
