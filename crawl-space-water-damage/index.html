Water Under the House Right Now

Crawl space water damage is water that collects in the low, unfinished space under a floor, then wets the insulation, vapor barrier, joists and subfloor above it. The cause is usually a plumbing leak or heavy rain finding its way in. If water is standing under your house tonight, the (301) 936-0619 line runs 24/7 and same-day response is offered when available.

Crawl spaces are one of several foundation types in Hyattsville. Some houses have full basements, some sit on slabs, and some, especially older ones, have a crawl space. If you are not sure which you have, look for a small access hatch outside or a panel in a closet floor.

First Safety Steps Before You Open the Hatch

A flooded crawl space is a confined space

Do not crawl into standing water. Pipes, wiring, ducts and sometimes gas lines run through the space, and you cannot see them under the surface. Shut the main water valve if a pipe may be leaking, and turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.

  • If water touches wiring, outlets or a furnace or water heater, stay out and call 911 and your electric utility before you call the line.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Washington Gas from outside. The leak line is 844-WASHGAS, option 1.
  • If the water has a sewage smell, keep everyone away and call WSSC Water at 301-206-4002. Sewage is grossly contaminated water.
  • Pipe or duct wrap in an older house may need testing before anyone disturbs it. Do not pull it down yourself.

What You See Above When the Crawl Space Is Wet

The first signs are often upstairs. Floors feel cold or spongy over one spot, and baseboards or hardwood may cup. Insulation hangs down from the joists, and a damp, earthy smell rises through floor registers.

Below, a flashlight from the hatch may show a glistening vapor barrier, dripping pipes or a visible waterline on the foundation wall. Any of these means moisture has been present for a while. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so an early call matters.

Why Hyattsville Crawl Spaces Take On Water

Water arrives from a few different directions, and the source decides the plan.

Plumbing leaks

A failed supply line, a leaking drain or a split pipe above the crawl space drips onto the soil. Clean-water leaks are easier to dry than sewage.

Heavy rain and poor drainage

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals at the nearest long-term station, Reagan National Airport, show 41.82 inches of precipitation a year. Downspouts that dump near the foundation send that water toward the vents and walls.

Aging street drainage

The city's own stormwater study says much of the storm drain system dates from 1960 to 1990, and that 41st Street experiences standing water during and after intense rain. A low spot like that sends runoff toward nearby foundations.

Major storm history

The county flood history page lists June 1972 Tropical Storm Agnes as the county's storm of record and names the Green Meadows subdivision in Hyattsville among the hardest hit. Check the FEMA flood map for your address at msc.fema.gov.

Condensation and open vents

Warm humid air meets cool surfaces under the house and drips. NOAA puts the July mean at 81.0 F, so summer moisture is a real load.

Pumping, Drying and Monitoring Under the Floor

  1. 1

    Stop the source and pump

    The plumbing is shut off, then a submersible pump moves standing water out through a hose. Discharge goes where it will not harm a neighbor, as the next section explains.

  2. 2

    Remove wet insulation

    Fiberglass batts that soaked up water sag and hold moisture against the subfloor. They are bagged and removed, and the vapor barrier is lifted if it traps water.

  3. 3

    Map and dry the structure

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera read the joists and subfloor. Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and sometimes HEPA air scrubbers dry the space and the floor above.

  4. 4

    Monitor until dry

    Readings are taken daily. The crawl space is not cleared until framing reads dry and the source is fixed.

Where the Pumped Water Is Allowed to Go

The city's resident sump pump brochure says sump pump water must not be directed onto a neighboring property or over the public right of way. The same sense applies to a pump hose from a flooded crawl space. A hose that runs across a sidewalk or into a neighbor's yard can create a new problem.

Point the discharge away from the foundation and onto your own lot. Prince George's County also publishes a Residential Drainage Manual that covers basement flooding, waterproofing, drainage systems and sump pumps. It is a useful read before you add a drain or a pump of your own.

What Changes the Size of a Crawl Space Job

Small clean-water leak

Scope: one section

A supply line drip, caught early, with a few wet batts and limited drying.

Standing clean water

Scope: full crawl space

Pumping, insulation removal and several days of drying under the house.

Rain or groundwater entry

Scope: contaminated space

Outside water is often significantly contaminated. Wet materials come out and cleaning follows.

Sewage or long-term moisture

Scope: structural

Contaminated materials, wet framing and a wet subfloor above add days and rebuild work.

Water category, space size, insulation type, access, drying days and rebuild scope set the price, and it is explained before work starts.

Insurance and the Crawl Space

Plumbing versus flooding

The Maryland Insurance Administration says most homeowners policies do not cover flooding, and sewer or water backup is an optional endorsement that insurers must offer. A sudden plumbing break is treated differently from slow seepage over time. Mold is excluded in most policies unless it results from a covered peril.

Read the water and flood sections of your policy, photograph the water and the damage before it is removed, and ask your insurer how it treats crawl space water. FEMA says a National Flood Insurance Program policy generally has a 30-day waiting period, so it must be in place before a storm, not after one.

Prevention and Mistakes to Avoid

  • ✓Extend downspouts away from the foundation so roof water does not collect at the crawl space wall.
  • ✓Check the grade. Soil that slopes toward the house sends rain to the vents and the wall.
  • ✓Ask about the vapor retarder. A U.S. Department of Energy job aid says a well-installed one helps minimize ground moisture vapor, and seams should overlap at least 12 inches.
  • ✓Do not run a fan or dehumidifier before the source is fixed. It dries air while the water keeps coming.
  • ✓Do not leave wet insulation in place. It holds water against the joists and subfloor.
  • ✓If mold appears on framing, see mold remediation and fix the water first.
