Stopping the damage in the first 72 hours

If water is spreading through your Hyattsville home right now, call (301) 936-0619. The line is open 24/7, and the crew sets priorities by how fast the water is moving. Water mitigation is the work of limiting a water loss: shutting off the source, removing standing water, setting drying equipment and tracking moisture until the structure is dry.

Mitigation is not the rebuild. Its job is to keep a bad day from becoming a bigger repair bill, because water wicks up drywall, soaks into subfloors and gets into wall cavities within hours. Once the building is dry, water damage repair deals with whatever could not be saved.

While you wait for the crew, the next sections cover what is safe to do yourself and what is not.

Wet carpet, a musty smell after a leak, a ceiling stain that keeps spreading

Those are the ways most people describe a wet building before they know what it is called. The trade term for fixing it is water mitigation, and it means stopping the water, pulling it out and drying the structure with measured equipment. A damp smell or a widening stain usually means moisture is still sitting somewhere you cannot see.

Water is spreading right now: do this while you wait

Acting early limits the damage, and the steps are simple. Stay safe first and save belongings second.

Safe steps before the crew arrives

  • ✓Stop the source if you can reach it safely: close the fixture valve, or shut off the main water valve for the house.
  • ✓Keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated, including anything from a sewer line or toilet.
  • ✓Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and move rugs, electronics and papers to a dry room.
  • ✓Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise call your electric utility.
  • ✓Take photos and video of the water, the source and every affected room before anything is moved.
  • ✓Open windows only if outdoor air is dry. In humid weather, closed doors and windows help the dehumidifiers work later.
  • ✓Call the line. A same-day response is possible when crews are available, including after hours and on weekends.

Wet outlets, gas smell or sewage: stay out first

Call the utility or 911 before you call the line

If water touches an electrical panel, outlet or cord, stay out of the room. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call your electric utility or 911 for any electrical risk.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas company from outside. Washington Gas lists its leak line as 844-WASHGAS (844-927-4427), option 1.

For a water main break or a sewer backup into the home, WSSC Water's 24-hour emergency line is 301-206-4002. Sewage is contaminated water, so keep everyone out of it.

Structural drying: equipment, readings and drying goals

Structural drying removes moisture from the building itself, not just the floor surface. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces. LGR dehumidifiers, which are low grain refrigerant units, pull that moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into the walls. Extraction units remove bulk water first, because it is faster to vacuum a puddle than to evaporate it.

Measurement guides all of it. Moisture meters read wood, drywall and concrete, and thermal imaging cameras show cool wet zones behind finished surfaces. A drying goal is set by comparing readings in the wet area against readings from a dry reference spot in the same building. Crews log the numbers each visit, which shows whether the drying is working.

Hyattsville's summers push against this work. The nearest long-term NOAA station, Reagan National Airport, shows a July mean of 81.0 F, and that warm, humid air carries moisture that slows drying. Dehumidifiers matter more in July than in January, whose mean is 37.5 F. The EPA's 24 to 48 hour guidance is the target, and a wet home in August needs a closed, dehumidified space to meet it.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference for professional water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.

What changes the size of a mitigation job

Clean-water leak, one room

Scope: small

A supply line or appliance leak caught quickly, with extraction and a few air movers.

Leak that reached the next room

Scope: moderate

Water crossed a doorway or soaked the ceiling below, so more rooms need mapping and drying.

Wet walls and subfloor

Scope: larger

Water got into wall cavities and under flooring, which means more equipment and more drying days.

Multi-level loss

Scope: extensive

Water ran through a floor system into the level below, with wet ceilings, walls and flooring on both.

Contaminated water

Scope: major

Sewage or floodwater changes the water category and what must be removed before drying begins.

Insurance and mitigation records

Keep moisture logs and photos

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and a gradual leak often is not. Flood damage is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage, and sewer backup often needs an endorsement. Check your policy or ask your insurer before you assume either way.

The drying logs, readings and photos from a mitigation job are the paper trail your insurer may ask to see. Ask the crew for copies when the job closes.
